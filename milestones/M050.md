## 1. Executive Summary

Milestone **M050** investigated the hypothesis that predicting **body-frame velocity vectors** ($v_{\text{forward}}, v_{\text{lateral}}$) via deep learning provides a more effective motion representation for downstream EKF fusion than scalar speed ($v_{\text{fwd}}$) alone, particularly during turning and braking maneuvers.

Four candidate multi-task models (F1–F4) were trained on 6-axis causal IMU inputs ($W=40$, 10 Hz) and evaluated through the exact M028 production EKF fusion engine across the validation partition ($88,566 \le k < 107,535$). Candidate **F1** (Body Velocity Network) achieved the lowest validation 300s navigation error of **107.48 m** (vs Control F0's $1248.83\text{ m}$) and was selected for locked test evaluation.

On the unseen locked test partition ($start\_idx = 108,000$), candidate **F1** failed to generalize, producing a 300s position error of **817.69 m** (+273.7% error increase compared to M028's **218.76 m**).

Consequently, **M050 is classified as a GENERALIZATION FAILURE**. Production pipeline remains **100% locked at M028**.

## 2. Experimental Setup & Target Definitions

### Target Transformation
Using reference VBOX Doppler speed $v_{\text{vbox}}$ and reference heading $\psi_{\text{vbox}}$:
1. **Forward Velocity Target** ($v_{\text{fwd\_gt}}$): Direct vehicle longitudinal speed $v_{\text{vbox}}$ (m/s).
2. **Lateral Velocity Target** ($v_{\text{lat\_gt}}$): Transverse lateral velocity derived from smoothed position derivative projected onto the vehicle lateral axis:
   $$v_{\text{lat\_gt}} = -v_{x, \text{pos}} \cos(\psi_{\text{vbox}}) + v_{y, \text{pos}} \sin(\psi_{\text{vbox}})$$
3. **Scalar Speed Target** ($s_{\text{gt}}$): $\sqrt{v_{\text{fwd\_gt}}^2 + v_{\text{lat\_gt}}^2}$.
4. **Longitudinal Acceleration Target** ($a_{\text{fwd\_gt}}$): Short-term velocity change over 1 second ($\Delta v$).

### Candidate Architecture Overview
- **F0 — Baseline Control**: Exact M028 SpeedNet v2 (W=40, 6-axis IMU).
- **F1 — Body Velocity Network**: Outputs $v_{\text{forward}}, v_{\text{lateral}}, \text{logit\_stat}$.
- **F2 — Multi-Task Velocity + Speed**: Outputs $v_{\text{forward}}, v_{\text{lateral}}, s, \text{logit\_stat}$.
- **F3 — Multi-Task Velocity + Acceleration**: Outputs $v_{\text{forward}}, v_{\text{lateral}}, a_{\text{forward}}, \text{logit\_stat}$.
- **F4 — Multi-Task Velocity + Speed + Acceleration**: Outputs $v_{\text{forward}}, v_{\text{lateral}}, s, a_{\text{forward}}, \text{logit\_stat}$.

## 6. Scientific Interpretation & Failure Analysis

1. **Why Validation Selection Failed**:
   In the validation sequence, candidate F1's learned forward velocity bias aligned fortuitously with validation trajectory turns, artificially canceling integrated drift down to 107.48 m. However, because F1 lacked scalar speed regularization, its forward velocity error on test doubled to 4.59 m/s, causing immediate navigation breakdown.

2. **Lateral Velocity Noise & EKF Coupling**:
   Predicting lateral velocity $v_{\text{lateral}}$ from 6-axis IMU without direct lateral orientation observability introduces noisy lateral velocity inputs. When fed into the EKF's non-holonomic constraint (NHC) residual engine, these noisy inputs disrupt the EKF's heading-cross-track self-damping geometry.

3. **Invariance of Geometric Self-Cancellation**:
   Scalar forward speed predictions ($v_{\text{fwd}}$) in SpeedNet v2 provide a stable longitudinal anchor. Attempting to replace scalar speed with unconstrained vector velocity branches destroys this geometric balance.
